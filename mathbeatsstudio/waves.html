Crest and Trough

A wave goes up and down. The top is the crest. The bottom is the trough. That is how a signal is drawn.

See it

Crest
say it: KREST (rhymes with best)
The top of the wave. The highest point above the middle line.
Trough
say it: TRAWF (rhymes with cough). The "ough" sounds like "off", not "oh".
The bottom of the wave. The lowest point below the middle line.
Rest line
The middle. Above it is plus (+). Below it is minus (−).

A signal is a wave that carries something: sound, light, a radio message. We draw it as crests and troughs so we can read it.

Four places to find them

The boat goes up to the crest, then down to the trough, again and again. The water itself does not travel to the shore. The shape of the wave does.

Slow motion: a real 300 Hz sound makes 300 crests every second. Where the dots squeeze together, the pressure is high: that is the crest. Where they spread out, it is low: that is the trough. A louder sound makes taller crests. A higher pitch packs more crests into the same space. Keep the volume down.

The rope is flicking by itself.

Press the left end of the rope and move up and down. Every bump you make travels along the rope. The tops get the word CREST and the bottoms get TROUGH.

In many places the sea rises and falls about twice a day. High tide is a crest. Low tide is a trough. This is an example picture, not a real tide chart.

How size and speed change the crest and trough

One second of a wave. Move the sliders and watch the crest, the trough and the count.

Amplitude
How far the crest is above the rest line. The trough is the same distance below. Bigger amplitude: higher crest and deeper trough, together. In sound it is loudness. In the ocean it is wave size.
Range
From the bottom of the trough up to the top of the crest. It is always twice the amplitude. Crest +3 and trough −3 make a range of 6.
Frequency
How many crests go by in one second, counted in hertz (Hz). Higher frequency: more crests and more troughs, but the same height. In sound it is pitch.
Period
The time from one crest to the next. It is 1 divided by the frequency. 4 crests every second means one crest every 0.25 seconds.
Frequency range
The lowest to the highest frequency something can make or hear. People hear about 20 to 20,000 Hz. The middle C on a piano is about 262 Hz.

Two different dials: amplitude moves the crest and trough up and down. Frequency packs more of them into each second. You can turn one without the other.

Questions kids ask

How do I say crest and trough?

Crest is KREST, like best. Trough is TRAWF, like cough. The "ough" in trough sounds like "off". Say it out loud three times: crest, trough, crest, trough.

Is the trough as big as the crest?

In a plain, smooth wave, yes. If the crest is 3 above the rest line, the trough is 3 below it. Real signals can be lopsided, but we start with the even ones.

Why is the middle line there?

It is the rest line: where the water, the rope or the air sits when nothing is moving. We measure every crest and trough from it. Above is plus. Below is minus.

Does a louder sound mean a taller crest?

Yes. Loudness is the height of the crest (and the depth of the trough). Quiet sounds make small waves. Loud sounds make tall ones.

What does a higher note change?

The number of crests per second. A high note has lots of crests packed close. A low note has fewer, spread far apart. Crests per second is called frequency, and we count it in hertz (Hz).

What is amplitude, and how is it different from frequency?

Amplitude is how tall the crest is (and how deep the trough is). Frequency is how many crests go by each second. A loud low note has a big amplitude and a low frequency. A quiet high note has a small amplitude and a high frequency.

What is range?

The distance from the bottom of the trough to the top of the crest. It is twice the amplitude. Some people call it peak to peak: crest is the peak on top, trough is the peak on the bottom.

What is a frequency range?

The span from the lowest to the highest frequency. People hear about 20 to 20,000 Hz, so that is our hearing range. A speaker or an instrument has a frequency range too.

What is a wavelength?

The distance from one crest to the next crest. Measure from trough to trough and you get the same answer.

Can a wave have just a crest?

One single bump is called a pulse, and a pulse can be only a crest. A wave that keeps going has both: crest, trough, crest, trough.

Why call it a signal?

A signal is a wave that carries a message. Your voice, a radio station and a phone call are all signals. Changing the crests and troughs is how the message gets in.

Where can I hear and see waves like this?

In the Math Beats Studio. Every track draws its curve in light and plays it as a note. Watch the curves rise to their crests and fall to their troughs.
