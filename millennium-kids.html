Seven questions worth $1,000,000 each

Start here. Some math questions are so hard that nobody on Earth knows the answer. In the year 2000, the Clay Mathematics Institute picked seven of them and offered $1,000,000 for each correct solution. Every one of them starts with something you already know: sharing cookies, solving a puzzle, a loop of string, flowing water, points on a graph, building with blocks, and steps on a ladder.

The Clay Institute announced the list on May 24, 2000, at the Collège de France in Paris. One has been solved so far (the Poincaré conjecture, number 3 below). The prize was offered to its solver and he turned it down.

How each chapter works: a hook you can try, what the question really asks, the history, and the equation written out one piece at a time. Orange tags mark a kid analogy. Blue tags mark the real math statement. An analogy helps you picture the idea; the real statement is what mathematicians have to prove.
