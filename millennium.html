Millennium Problems Field Guide

Seven $1M problems: the theory, the open issue, the sound link

The board, as of 21 Nov 2026

In 2000 the Clay Mathematics Institute put $1,000,000 on each of seven problems. One is solved (Poincaré). One, Riemann, got a proof on 20 Nov 2026 that the Lean and Mathlib communities certified as machine-checked correct on 21 Nov. Five are wide open.

How the prize actually pays

  • The proof has to be published in a qualifying journal.
  • Then a two-year wait.
  • Then "general acceptance" by the math community. You can't submit to Clay directly.

So even the Riemann proof, certified in Lean on 21 Nov 2026, can't pay out before late 2028, and only after journal publication and acceptance by mathematicians.

Riemann Hypothesis Proof certified in Lean · 21 Nov 2026

The primes look random. Riemann found a function whose zeros control exactly how they're spread out. The question: do all those zeros sit on one straight line?

ζ(s) = 1 + 1/2s + 1/3s + 1/4s + … = ∏primes p 1/(1 − p−s) Left: a sum over every whole number. Right: a product over only the primes. That equals sign (Euler, 1737) is why zeta "knows" the primes.
Riemann Hypothesis: every nontrivial zero has the form   s = ½ + iγ Real part exactly ½: the "critical line." Over twelve trillion zeros have been checked and all are on it. A proof has to cover the infinitely many that are left.

See where the zeros live: the Hardy Z curve

Window: t = 10–60

Z(t) is a real-valued curve that is ±|ζ(½ + it)|. Every time it crosses zero, that's a zeta zero sitting exactly on the critical line. Gold dots = the 10,025 stored zeros from the table at the bottom. The curve is computed live with the Riemann–Siegel formula, so the dots and the crossings are two independent calculations that agree. Slide the window up to t ≈ 9,900 and they keep agreeing. That's evidence, not proof.

Chord / one by one: zero n becomes the tone root × γn/γ1, set against a normal harmonic series (root × n). A real instrument sounds smooth; the zeros don't. That tone mapping is a sonification choice, not physics. Tones above 1,800 Hz are dropped and volume is capped. Headphones or a phone speaker at low volume.

The theory

  • Each zero γ adds a wave √x · cos(γ·ln x) to the prime count. The ½ in "½ + iγ" sets how loud the wave can get: √x.
  • If any zero sat off the line (real part above ½), its wave would grow faster than √x and the primes would be lumpier than we think.
  • RH true means the primes are as evenly spread as they can possibly be.

The issue

  • Checking zeros one at a time never ends. You need an argument that covers all of them at once.
  • Aug 2026: Claude pushed the proven share of zeros on the line from 41.6% to 67.2%. A big jump, but not 100%.
  • 20 Nov 2026: Levent Alpöge (Anthropic) released a proof made with Claude as a Lean library of about 17 million lines. It covers RH and the generalized version.
  • 21 Nov 2026: the Lean and Mathlib communities, endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative, certified that both the statement and the proof are correct. They checked that it compiles, states the right theorem and passes independent proof checkers.
  • What's still open: a machine check is not human understanding. The written version reportedly runs over 250,000 pages, and mathematicians still have to digest and simplify it. Clay's prize also needs journal publication plus two years. A separate all-human proof of the generalized version, from a Stanford postdoc, is still a paper waiting on review.

Hum link (real, not metaphor)

The spacing between zeta zeros statistically matches the energy levels of heavy atomic nuclei, and the explicit formula is literally a Fourier sum, the same math as breaking a sound into pure tones. Of the seven, this is the strongest genuine bridge between math and vibration.

Start from step 1. Every time.

  1. Primes build the zeta function (Euler: a sum over all whole numbers equals a product over primes).
  2. Its zeros are the inputs where it equals zero. The first is near 14.13.
  3. RH says every nontrivial zero sits on one line.
  4. The Hilbert–Pólya hunch: the zeros are the "notes" of a system whose notes are always real numbers. That would put them on the line. The system is the operator. Nobody has found one.

The wobble is made of primes

Count the zeros up to t. Subtract the smooth count. What is left, S(t), is the wobble a real operator must produce. A heuristic formula builds it from primes: one sine per prime power, with periods set by ln p.

navy = true wobble S(t)red = rebuilt from primes
Slide the window up to t = 9,800 and watch the match. Measured: with primes up to 100 the match is 0.89 near t = 10, 0.80 near 1,000 and 0.75 near 9,800. With primes up to 2,000 it is 0.93, 0.89 and 0.85. The wobble needs more primes at height. This is the Gutzwiller / Berry–Keating idea: each prime is an orbit of length ln p. It is the real reason the zeros are tied to music: they are a spectrum, and primes are the loops that spectrum rings from. Heuristic, not proved.

Hear the Proof Real data

A Lean proof is a tower of facts. Each file uses the ones under it. Pull one out and everything that leans on it stops checking. This is the real tower under the Riemann zeta function in Mathlib, Lean's shared math library.

What you're hearing

  • Each circle is a field of math. Each dot is one file of proven facts, sized by how many lines it has. Gold = everything that falls.
  • Colour → sound, honestly: each field is given a real visible-light frequency (430–750 THz), and its pitch is that light frequency dropped exactly 40 octaves (÷ 240), which lands at 391–682 Hz. Tap a chip to hear a colour.
  • Build: foundations play first, two octaves low. Each layer climbs higher until the zeta function sits on top, then every field sounds at once.
  • Fall: the pulled file sounds and cuts out. Then every file above it drops an octave and goes silent, in the order the proof stacks them.

Sonification The structure is real data. The mapping to sound (light ÷ 240, layer → octave, pluck per file) is a design choice. The dots aren't frequencies or energies of anything physical. They are facts.

P vs NP Open

If a computer can check an answer fast, can it always find one fast?

P  ⊆  NP     Question: P = NP ? P = problems you can solve in polynomial time (n², n³…). NP = problems where you can check a proposed answer in polynomial time.

See it: check vs. search

Items n: 20
Check an answer ≈ n stepsn³ (still "easy")Brute-force search 2ⁿ

Hear it

Sonification Each step adds one item. Low thumps = checking (n of them). High ticks = brute-force search (2ⁿ of them), crammed into the same time slot. By step 9 the ticks blur into a roar. That roar is exponential growth, and P vs NP asks whether you can always avoid it.

The theory

  • Example, subset sum: given n numbers, is there a group that adds to exactly 100? Checking a proposed group: add it up. Finding one: in the worst case, try up to 2ⁿ groups.
  • Thousands of real problems (routing, scheduling, packing, protein folding variants) are "NP-complete." Crack one fast and you've cracked all of them.

The issue

  • Nobody can prove the fast method doesn't exist. Proving "no algorithm works" means ruling out every possible algorithm, including ones nobody has thought of.
  • Several known proof techniques have been formally shown unable to settle it ("barriers"). That's a big reason it's stuck.
  • Most experts bet P ≠ NP. If P = NP, most of modern encryption breaks.

Link to this week

An analogy, not a link: the Riemann proof took huge effort to find, while checking it in Lean took the community about a day. That echoes find vs. check, but it says nothing about P vs NP itself.

Our odds: near zero. Widely seen as the deepest of the seven.

Navier–Stokes Open

These equations run every weather model and aircraft design. Can a smooth flow, left alone, ever develop a point of infinite speed (a "blow-up")? Or does it always stay smooth?

∂u/∂t + (u·∇)u = −∇p + ν∇²u,    ∇·u = 0 u = velocity at each point. (u·∇)u = the flow carrying itself along (makes swirls sharper). ν∇²u = viscosity (smooths swirls out). ∇·u = 0 means incompressible.

See it: a real 2D flow solve

Viscosity ν:

Sonification Live: the spin along the middle row of the grid becomes the shape of one sound wave, repeated at 110 Hz and updated as the flow evolves. Sharp filaments = buzzy, bright tone. Raise ν and the flow smooths out, so the tone goes mellow. You're hearing the solver itself, not a recording.

This is the real equation, not a cartoon: 2D incompressible Navier–Stokes in vorticity form (∂ω/∂t + u·∇ω = ν∇²ω), solved pseudo-spectrally on a 128×128 periodic grid with FFTs, 2/3-rule dealiasing and integrating-factor SSP-RK3 time steps. Blue/red = spin direction. The lowest ν is set by what a 128×128 grid can resolve honestly; go lower and the grid itself starts ringing. Low ν keeps razor-thin filaments; high ν smears them out and energy drains away. Why no prize here: in 2D it's proven the flow stays smooth forever. The open 3D case adds vortex stretching, the term that could make spin spike, and 2D simply doesn't have it.

The theory

  • In 2D, it's proven: flows stay smooth forever.
  • In 3D, vortex lines can stretch, and stretching spins them faster. That's the term nobody can control.

The issue

  • The energy bounds we have are too weak to rule out a spike. They're "supercritical": they get weaker exactly at small scales, where blow-up would happen.
  • The prize pays either way: prove it always stays smooth, or build one example that blows up.
  • Active front: computer-assisted searches for blow-up candidates in related equations. That's the angle where AI-plus-human work is moving.

Hum link (with a gotcha)

It's the physics of air and breath. But ∇·u = 0 (incompressible) is exactly the assumption that removes sound waves, because sound is air compressing. So the prize version is the breath, not the hum.

Our odds: near zero, but it's the most "physical" one and has the most computer-search traction.

Yang–Mills & the Mass Gap Open

The theory behind the forces of nature works brilliantly in experiments, but nobody has built it rigorously in math. Prove it exists, and prove its lightest particle has mass greater than zero (the "gap").

ℒ = −¼ Faμν Faμν,    F = dA + A∧A Electromagnetism (light) is the simple version without the A∧A term. In the strong force, that extra term makes the force carriers (gluons) interact with each other.

See it: why a mass gap makes a force short-range

Mass gap m: 1.0
Light / photon: 1/r, reaches foreverMassive carrier: e−mr/r, dies off at range ≈ 1/m

Slide m to 0 and the red curve becomes the gold one: no gap, so the force reaches forever like light. Any m above 0 chokes the force off at a distance. The gap is why the strong force holds a nucleus together and is never felt outside it.

Hear it

Real physics (pitch scaled to be audible). A particle's frequency obeys ω = √(k² + m²). First pass, m = 0 (light): as k drops, the pitch slides all the way down to nothing, so there's no lowest note. Second pass, at your slider's m: the pitch slides down and stops at a floor. That floor is what a mass gap looks like for one free particle. The prize is harder: it asks for a proof that the interacting strong-force theory has a floor above zero.

The theory

  • Physicists use the theory daily, and it predicts particle collider results to many decimal places.
  • But the math underneath (an integral over all possible field shapes) has never been rigorously defined in 4D spacetime.

The issue

  • Two hard parts: (1) build the theory to full mathematical standard, (2) prove the gap.
  • Computer simulations on a spacetime grid ("lattice QCD") show the gap. A simulation isn't a proof.

Light link

This is the "light" problem. The photon is the massless, gapless case: light travels across the universe because there is no gap. The prize asks why the strong force's version of light can't do the same.

Our odds: near zero. Needs heavy physics and analysis background.

Hodge Conjecture Open

Complicated high-dimensional shapes have "holes" of different types. Can every hole of a certain special kind be built from pieces cut out by simple polynomial equations?

Hdgk(X) = H2k(X,ℚ) ∩ Hk,k(X)   is spanned by algebraic cycles Plain version: the "special" holes (Hodge classes) are exactly the ones you can build from shapes defined by equations (algebraic cycles), using rational amounts.

See it: harmonic modes, the engine of Hodge theory

Mode m: 2   Mode n: 3

A square drumhead vibrating in mode (m, n). The dark lines are nodes, places that never move (Chladni sand collects there). Hodge's key move: on any shape, every "hole" has exactly one most-relaxed harmonic form, solving Δω = 0 with the same Laplacian Δ that governs this drum.

Hear it

Real physics A square drum's mode (m, n) rings at a frequency proportional to √(m² + n²). First you hear your chosen mode alone. Then a full strike: 49 modes at once, each one decaying. Those frequencies aren't whole-number multiples of each other, which is why drums clang instead of singing. Same Laplacian, same harmonic math Hodge theory runs on.

The theory

  • Hodge proved every hole has a unique "harmonic" representative, the smoothest possible wave pattern living on it.
  • Those harmonic patterns split into types (p, q). The conjecture says the (k, k) type always comes from honest geometry.

The issue

  • Proven in low dimensions (the Lefschetz (1,1) theorem). Wide open beyond that.
  • Known to fail if you relax the rules (integer coefficients, or non-algebraic shapes), so a proof has to use the exact setup.
  • The most abstract of the seven. Hard to even state without graduate geometry.

Hum link

The word "harmonic" is literal here: it's the math of standing waves and resonance. It's an honest bridge to the hum episode, but only as an illustration.

Our odds: near zero. Prerequisites are years deep.

Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer Open

Take a curve like y² = x³ + ax + b. Does it have finitely or infinitely many points with fraction coordinates? BSD says a zeta-like function tells you, just by how it behaves at one spot.

rank E(ℚ) = ords=1 L(E, s),    L(E,s) ≈ ∏p (1 − app−s + p1−2s)−1 Rank = how many independent "seed" points generate all the rational points. L(E, s) is built prime by prime, just like zeta.

The curve + rational points found

The 1960s experiment, rerun live

a: -1 b: 1

Right panel: for each prime p, count the curve's points mod p (Np), and multiply up Np/p. Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer saw in 1960s computer runs that this product grows like (log P)rank. Curves with more rational points have more points mod p, so the product climbs. Rank 0 curves stay flat.

Hear it

Sonification One note per prime p. Pitch = how far the curve's point count mod p lands from p + 1 (higher = more points than expected). The low drone follows the same running product ∏ Nₚ/p as the graph on the right. On rank 0 curves it barely moves. On rank 1 curves it climbs, and how far depends on this page's pitch mapping. That is the BSD pattern, by ear. Try the presets.

The issue

  • Proven when the rank is 0 or 1 (Gross–Zagier, Kolyvagin). Open for rank 2 and up.
  • The full conjecture also predicts an exact formula for the leading coefficient, with some pieces (like the "Sha" group) nobody can even prove are finite in general.

Hum link

L(E, s) is a cousin of zeta, built from one "note" per prime. This is the same family of music as Riemann, just playing a different curve.

Our odds: near zero. But the experiment on the right is real math you can run, and it's how the conjecture was discovered.

Poincaré Conjecture Solved · Perelman 2003

If every loop in a closed 3D space can be shrunk to a point, is that space just a (3D) sphere in disguise?

∂g/∂t = −2 Ric(g)    (Ricci flow) Let the shape's geometry g flow: bulges shrink, dents fill in, like heat evening out. Perelman showed the flow (with careful "surgery" at pinch points) always reveals the true shape.

See it: the loop test

On a sphere, any loop slides off and shrinks to a point. On a donut, a loop around the hole gets stuck. Poincaré asked whether "every loop shrinks" pins down the sphere in 3D. Answer: yes.

Hear it

Sonification built on real heat-flow math. The tone starts "bumpy," with 36 random overtones like a lumpy shape. Each overtone k fades like e−k²t, the way heat (and Ricci flow) irons out small wrinkles fastest. What's left is one pure tone: the round sphere. Perelman's proof is this idea, done in 3D with surgery at the pinch points.

How it was solved

  • Richard Hamilton invented Ricci flow (1982). Grigori Perelman finished the program in three online papers (2002–03), never published in a journal.
  • Clay awarded him the $1M in 2010. He declined it, and the Fields Medal too.

Takeaway for us

The one solved problem fell to a smoothing flow, the same "settle into the relaxed state" idea as heat, harmonics and resonance. It also shows the prize rules bend: Perelman never published in a journal and was still recognized.

Status: done. Nothing left to win.
