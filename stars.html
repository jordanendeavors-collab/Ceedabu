The Four Watchers

Around 3000 BC, four bright stars sat close to the equinoxes and solstices: the ox, the lion, the eagle and the man of Ezekiel 1:10, if you follow the old tradition. Earth's slow wobble has turned the sky about 70° since. Drag the years and watch the cross drift off the stars.

3000 BC

    Outer ring: the real constellations, fixed to the stars, so they turn. Inner ring: the astrological signs, fixed to the equinox, so they stay put. Today the spring equinox sits in Pisces, not Aries. Matching the four creatures to these stars is a later tradition; Ezekiel names the faces, not the stars.

    How the sound is made

    Stars are AM transmitters

    Pressure waves bounce around inside a star. The surface swells and dims slightly in rhythm, so the starlight is a carrier modulated by the star's own acoustics. Kepler and TESS measured this on thousands of stars.

    Physics sets the pitch

    Peak ring frequency scales with surface gravity over the square root of temperature (νmax), and mode spacing scales with mean density (Δν). Both are calibrated on the Sun: 3,090 μHz and 135 μHz.

    Then it's shifted up

    Real periods run from minutes to months, so every star is sped up by millions to trillions of times. Pitch spread is log-compressed into five octaves. Each star's own mode spacing is kept, and so is its rank: higher means denser.

    Star distances, temperatures, radii and masses are rounded published values. Several (Deneb, Alnilam, Betelgeuse) are still debated. Works offline.
